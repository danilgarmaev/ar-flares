=======================
General/Open Questions
=======================

[-1] Me: In this labeling problem, there is a fuzzy transition zone between
class C and class M. Would it have been worthwhile to consider a fuzzy
class label that fell halfway between the two and modeled the
uncertainty between these two classes?
Please comment.

Response:
This is an interesting suggestion, especially from the perspective of ordinal or uncertainty-aware prediction. In the present thesis, we kept the benchmark formulation used in the dataset and in prior flare-prediction work, namely binary threshold tasks ($\geq C$ and $\geq M$), in order to maintain comparability and isolate input-design effects. A fuzzy or ordinal labeling scheme could be worthwhile in future work, but it would change the prediction problem itself and would require a different evaluation framework. We therefore view it as a valuable extension rather than as a missing component of the current controlled study.

[-2] Me:  Among the four metrics used in the article to quantify the quality of
the classification (F-measure, TSS, AUC, PR-AUC), Why not calculate
the pairwise correlation of all the results from these measures (6
possible combinations) to determine which were most correlated and
which were least correlated, in order to ultimately identify which 2
or 3 measures were the most complementary?
Please comment.

Response:
This would indeed be a useful supplementary analysis, especially for understanding metric redundancy and complementarity. In the current thesis, we instead chose the metrics based on their distinct interpretive roles: TSS for operating-point skill under imbalance, AUC and PR-AUC for threshold-free ranking, and F1 for the precision-recall trade-off at the selected threshold. Because the main focus of the thesis is controlled input-design analysis rather than metric selection, we did not include a full metric-correlation study. We agree, however, that such an analysis would be valuable in a broader benchmarking paper or extended follow-up study.
